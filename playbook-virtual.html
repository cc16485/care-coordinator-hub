Facebook ads for virtual caregiver visits

Three ads, nine images, and the settings to run them. Everything here points at mo-care.com/hometogether, where the visit builder does the selling.

Read this first, or the ads get rejected

Never write "you" or "your mother" in a way that implies we know something about the person reading. Meta's personal attributes policy forbids ads that assert or imply knowledge of somebody's health, condition, or circumstances, and elder care trips it constantly. This is the single most common reason an ad like this gets disapproved.
Rejected: "Caring for a parent with dementia?" · "Worried about your mom living alone?" · "Is your father forgetting his medications?"
Fine: "Most families do not need somebody in the house all day." · "Caring Companions caregivers check in by video." · "Dorothy's television comes on by itself."
Every ad below is written in the third person for exactly this reason. If you rewrite one, keep it describing us or a family, never you.
The other thing that changed: Meta removed health-related interest targeting in 2022, so you cannot target "Alzheimer's disease" or "caregiving" as interests any more. Do not go hunting for them. Broad targeting with a good creative now beats clever targeting, which is genuinely how it works today.

The three ads

Run all three at once in one ad set and let Meta find the winner. Do not run them in separate ad sets, that splits the learning and costs more to get nowhere.

Ad 1. The momentStart here

Shows the thing no competitor shows: what actually happens in a visit. Works because it is specific. “Show me the plate” is not something a stock-photo agency would ever write.

Primary text
At nine in the morning, Dorothy's television comes on by itself. Denise is already there. She asks whether the blue pill went down with breakfast. Then she asks to see the plate. Not "did you eat," but show me. That is the difference between a phone call and a visit. Caring Companions caregivers make video check-ins on the television that is already in the living room. Every weekday. Medications watched, meals checked, and somebody who notices when a day looks different from the last one. From $75 a week, which is less than one four-hour in-home visit.
Headline
A caregiver looks in every weekday
Description
From $75 a week. Springfield and southwest Missouri.
Ad 2. The priceBest for cold traffic

The comparison is the argument. Put a number next to a number and let people do the arithmetic themselves.

Primary text
In-home care comes in four-hour blocks. Around here that is roughly $120 for one afternoon. A whole week of daily video check-ins from a Caring Companions caregiver starts at $75. Five days of somebody looking in, for less than one afternoon of somebody sitting. Medications watched, meals checked, and a real conversation, on the television already in the living room. Pick what each visit should cover and the price works itself out.
Headline
Five days of care for less than one visit
Description
Daily video check-ins from $75 a week.
Ad 3. The gapBest for retargeting

Names the objection nobody says out loud. People who have priced home care and walked away will recognise themselves in this one.

Primary text
Most families do not need somebody in the house all day. They need somebody there at eight in the morning when the pills matter, and again at six when the day gets long. But in-home care comes in four-hour blocks, so a family who needs twenty minutes twice a day pays for eight hours or goes without. Almost everyone goes without. And then worries all day. Caring Companions virtual caregiver visits are built for exactly that gap. A real caregiver, on the television, every weekday, from $75 a week.
Headline
Nobody needs four hours
Description
Fifteen minutes, twice a day, from $75 a week.

Setting it up

Campaign

  • Objective: Leads if you want Meta optimising for form fills, or Traffic to start cheap and see whether anyone bites. Traffic first is the honest choice for a new offer.
  • One campaign, one ad set, three ads.
  • Advantage campaign budget on.

Audience

  • Location: Springfield plus 25 miles. Add Nixa, Ozark, Republic, Bolivar, Branson and Willard by name if the radius misses them.
  • Age: 45 to 70. The buyer is the adult child, not the person receiving care.
  • Gender: all. Daughters buy more of this than sons, but let the algorithm find that out rather than deciding it for it.
  • Detailed targeting: leave it empty. See the note above about health interests being gone. Broad plus good creative is the play.
  • Advantage+ audience: on.

Before you press go

  • Meta pixel firing on mo-care.com. Without it you are flying blind and cannot retarget.
  • Whoever answers the phone knows this offer exists and what it costs. An ad that works and a coordinator who says "let me find out" wastes the money.
  • Decide now who calls a lead back and how fast. Same day, or the ad is a donation.

What to watch, and what to ignore

NumberWhat it meansWhat to do
Cost per clickUnder about $1.50 is healthy for this audience.Above $3, the creative is not landing. Change the image before the words.
Click-through rateOver 1% means the ad is doing its job.Under 0.6% after 5,000 impressions, kill that ad and put the budget on the other two.
Cost per leadThe only number that matters.Judge it against what a client is worth, not against what a click costs.
Likes and sharesAlmost nothing.Ignore. A quiet ad that produces three calls beats a popular one that produces none.
The comment you will get, and the answer: somebody will write "so it's just a video call?" under the ad, probably in the first week. Reply publicly and plainly: it is a caregiver, at a set time, who watches the medications go down and tells the family what they saw. A phone call cannot do any of that. Answering it in the comments is free advertising, and leaving it there costs you the next ten readers.
Turn the ads off if you cannot staff the visits. Nothing costs more than a family who calls, hears yes, and then waits. Check capacity before you scale the budget, not after.
