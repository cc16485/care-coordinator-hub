Who pays for care?

Every way a family can pay us, in one place. These are the same choices as "Who's paying" on a lead. Pick one above for the details.

This is your job

Care Coordinators are responsible for knowing all of these programs: private pay, long-term care insurance, VA, and Medicare GUIDE. When a family calls, you are the person who finds every way their care can be paid for and walks them through signing up, from the first call to the first visit. Families should never have to figure out the paperwork alone.

  • Know who qualifies, what each program pays, and the steps to get started (each tab above).
  • Ask the five questions below on every first call, so no family misses a program they could use.
  • Never make a family wait on a program. Care can start as private pay while GUIDE, LTC or VA paperwork goes through.
  • Never promise approval, hours or a start date until the program has confirmed it.

Five questions on every first call

  1. "Did your loved one serve in the military, or were they married to someone who did?" Yes: look at VA. Community Care if they get care through the VA, Aid & Attendance if not.
  2. "Do they have a long-term care insurance policy?" Yes: ask them to find the policy and look at LTC insurance.
  3. "Do they have a dementia or Alzheimer's diagnosis?" and "Is their Medicare the red, white and blue card, or a Medicare Advantage plan?" Diagnosis plus traditional Medicare: screen them for GUIDE.
  4. "Do they have Medicaid?" Active Medicaid and they want to choose their own caregiver: hand off to Caring CDS.
  5. Everyone else, or for hours a program doesn't cover: private pay. Many families use two at once, for example GUIDE visits plus private-pay hours.

A Medicaid caller? We no longer take agency Medicaid. Send them to Caring Companions CDS, (417) 218-2888.
Dementia and traditional Medicare? Screen them for GUIDE on the GUIDE page, even if they are paying privately too.

Getting a private-pay family started

  3. Book the in-home assessmentSet one dated next step before you hang up. Bring the assessment folder (Rev. 10/2026).

Who qualifies

  • Dementia diagnosis
  • Traditional Medicare, not Medicare Advantage
  • Lives in their own home, a family member's home, or independent living. Not assisted living, memory care, or a nursing home.
  • Not on hospice and not in PACE (palliative care is fine)
  • Has an unpaid caregiver
  • Medicaid and VA alongside it are fine

Getting a GUIDE family started

  1. Screen on the phoneUse the screener on the GUIDE page. Ask about the diagnosis and Medicare Advantage first, since those rule out the most people. Save it to the screening log. "Based on what you've told me, they may qualify. Medicare decides, and I'll walk you through every step." Never say "free care" or "you're approved."
  2. Collect what Crosby needsPatient name, date of birth, Medicare number (MBI), address, phone, and the unpaid caregiver's name, relationship and phone. The screener builds the summary for you.
  3. Submit in Crosby Health by Friday noonAlignment comes back the Tuesday about 11 days later. Steps are on the GUIDE page under Crosby Health.
  4. Don't make them waitIf they need help now, start private pay and switch the visits over once they align.
  5. Do the home assessmentThe caregiver answers the FAST questions (never the patient), fills in the ZBI alone, and must be named as the unpaid caregiver. Don't rush or repeat the ten-word list.
  6. Respite decisionCrosby shows Pending or Not Eligible. Only FAST stage 5 to 7 with a named unpaid caregiver gets respite dollars. Everyone enrolled still gets care navigation and the 24/7 line.
  7. Staff it with a GUIDE-trained caregiverMedicare won't pay for a visit by a caregiver without the GUIDE training on file. Tell staffing it's a GUIDE client.
  8. Bill and trackBill visits in Crosby. Keep count of the 20 visits; they reset July 1 and don't roll over.

Long-term care insurance

Taking new clients

Billed at our private-pay rates. A policy can cover most or all of the cost once it starts paying. We verify benefits, set up a call with the carrier, help with the claim forms, and can bill the carrier directly.

When a policy starts paying

Usually once the person needs help with 2 of these 6, or has a cognitive impairment such as dementia: bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, continence. A licensed professional certifies it. That is the policy's "benefit trigger."

Two ways to bill

  • Direct billing. The family signs an assignment of benefits and we bill the carrier. Within the policy limits, the family pays nothing.
  • Pay, then get reimbursed. The family pays our rate and we give them itemized paid invoices and paperwork to send in.

Words on every policy

  • Elimination period. Days the family pays before benefits begin, often 30, 60 or 90.
  • Daily or monthly maximum. The most the policy pays. Care above it is the family's.
  • Benefit period. How long benefits last, or the total the policy will pay.
  • Inflation protection. Grows the daily benefit over time.

Ask the family to have the policy out at the home assessment. Handout: folder piece 9.

Getting an LTC insurance family started

  1. Get the policyAsk them to upload it on the LTC page at mo-care.com, email it to intake@mo-care.com, or have it out at the assessment. Ask for the policy number and the carrier's claims phone number.
  2. Read it before you talk moneyPaste it into the LTC Policy tab. Find: does it cover a home care agency, the elimination period, the daily or monthly maximum, the benefit trigger, and whether it allows assignment of benefits (direct billing).
  3. Call the carrier with the familyA three-way call with the policyholder or their power of attorney on the line, since carriers only talk to us with permission. Confirm benefits and open the claim. Write down the claim number, the name of who you spoke to, and the date.
  4. Help with the claim formsThe carrier usually sends a claim packet: a form for the family, one for the doctor or a licensed professional who certifies the need for care, and one for us as the provider. Help the family fill in their part and get the doctor's part back.
  5. Start care nowDuring the elimination period the family pays our private-pay rate. Sign the Client Care Agreement as usual and keep every invoice; carriers often ask for proof of those days.
  6. Pick how we billDirect billing (the family signs the assignment of benefits) or the family pays and sends our invoices in. Set the carrier up as a payor in AxisCare (steps on the LTC Policy tab).
  7. Keep it paidSend invoices and visit notes on the carrier's schedule. Watch for recertification dates and anything above the daily maximum, which is the family's to pay. Tell the family before they reach the policy's limit.

Have the policy in hand? The hub's LTC Policy tab reads it for you, walks through setting the carrier up as a payor in AxisCare, and has our claim steps.

VA

Taking new clients

Two different paths. Find out which one the family means before anything else.

VA Community Care (the VA pays us)

  • Needs an active VA authorization number. We give only the hours and type of service on it.
  • Needs a plan of care from, or approved by, the VA.
  • EVV is required. No clock-in, no VA payment.
  • No transportation. Caregivers cannot drive VA Community Care clients anywhere.
  • No holiday rate, mileage or overtime. The VA never pays them, and we never bill the veteran for them.
  • Extra hours or tasks outside the authorization are private pay.
  • If the authorization ends, is cut or is denied, the family can carry on as private pay.
  • The family signs the VA Community Care Addendum as well as the agreement.

Aid & Attendance (the family pays us)

A monthly, tax-free payment added to a VA pension. The money is the veteran's, so to us this is private pay.

  • Wartime-era service, or a surviving spouse who hasn't remarried
  • Meets at least one of these: needs another person's help with daily activities like bathing, dressing or eating; stays in bed much of the day because of illness; lives in a nursing home because of a physical or mental condition; or has very limited eyesight
  • Net worth under the VA limit (about $163,700 in 2026, not counting the home, one vehicle and belongings)
  • The VA looks back 3 years at asset transfers

A Veterans Service Officer helps them apply for free. Handout: folder piece 9.

Getting a VA Community Care veteran started

We are in network with the VA Community Care Network. The VA pays us directly. It starts with a referral from the veteran's VA doctor.

  1. Check they get care through the VAAsk if the veteran has a VA primary care doctor or care team. If not, they need to enroll in VA health care first (VA.gov or 877-222-8387).
  2. Ask who they want as their caregiverMany veterans don't want a stranger. A family member or friend can be hired by us as their paid caregiver: 18 or older, no paid experience needed, background checks and orientation still required. Get the veteran's VA case manager to OK a family member or friend in writing before they start. Send them to mo-care.com/apply?position=va_family_caregiver. The office calls them first. We pay weekly.
  3. Get the referral goingThe veteran or family asks the VA primary care team for a Homemaker and Home Health Aide (H/HHA) referral to Caring Companions. Offer to help: go through the request with them on the phone, then fax, send or mail it for them. "You don't pay Caring Companions for care the VA authorizes. Some veterans have a VA copay, which the VA would tell you about."
  4. Wait for the authorizationNo VA-paid visit until the written authorization arrives, usually 3 to 14 days. It normally comes through HSRM, the VA's referral website, and sometimes by fax, email or a call from the VA case manager. It lists the authorization number, the hours and the type of service. That is all we can give on the VA. If they need help sooner, offer private pay until it arrives.
  5. PaperworkClient Care Agreement plus the VA Community Care Addendum. A plan of care from, or approved by, the VA.
  6. Brief staffingEVV clock-in and clock-out every visit (no clock-in, no VA payment). No driving the veteran anywhere. Only the authorized hours and tasks; anything extra is private pay. The VA week runs Sunday to Saturday, and unused hours don't roll over to the next week.
  7. Watch the end dateAuthorizations usually last 60 to 90 days. Put the end date on the client's record. Ask the VA case manager for a renewal authorization well before it runs out, with care notes showing the need continues. Each case manager wants a different amount of notice, so ask them and write it on the record. Never give VA care on a lapsed authorization. If it ends or is cut, the family can carry on as private pay.
  8. Billing goes through ParadigmWe bill the VA for Community Care visits through Paradigm. Visits need the EVV clock-in and clock-out, care notes that match, and the authorization number to be paid. Claims must go in within 180 days, and the billing code must match the one on the authorization. Cancelled or missed shifts can't be billed. Payment usually takes 15 to 30 days.

Helping an Aid & Attendance family

  1. Start care as private payA&A can take months. The family pays us now; the VA can pay benefits back to the date they filed.
  2. Send them to a Veterans Service OfficerVSOs help them apply for free. We don't fill in or file VA claims for families, and nobody should charge them to apply.
  3. Give them what the VA asks for from usOur signed Client Care Agreement and paid invoices show the cost of care. Their doctor fills in VA Form 21-2680 about the help they need.

Still to confirm with Samantha

  • Who on the team has an HSRM sign-in and watches it for new and renewed authorizations. Each person must sign in at least once every 35 days or they lose access.
  • Who on the team sends VA claims in Paradigm, and what they need from the coordinator.
  • Who asks the VA case manager to renew an authorization.

CDS (Consumer Directed Services)

Through Caring CDS

Missouri Medicaid where the family hires their own caregiver. This runs through our sister company, Caring Companions CDS, not through this hub.

Send them here

  • Phone: (417) 218-2888, toll-free (866) 863-5151
  • Website: caringcds.com
  • CDS office hub: hub.caringcds.com

Good to know

  • CDS covers a wider area than we do, 27 counties across Southwest Missouri.
  • The CDS caregivers work for the family, not for Caring Companions.
  • Clock-in is by the WellSky app or by phone from the client's home.

Medicaid IHS and ADW

Ending November 16, 2026

From November 16, 2026, Caring Companions no longer provides agency-model Missouri Medicaid: In-Home Services (IHS) and the Aged & Disabled Waiver (ADW). CDS carries on through Caring CDS.

  • No new IHS or ADW clients. Send Medicaid callers to Caring Companions CDS, (417) 218-2888.
  • Each remaining IHS or ADW client is either ending service or moving to Caring CDS.
  • State rule: when a provider can no longer meet a participant's needs, written notice of discharge goes to the participant and the state at least 21 days ahead, and care continues for those 21 days or until other care is arranged (19 CSR 15-7.021(16)(D)).
